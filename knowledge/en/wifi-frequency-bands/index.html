Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz vs 6 GHz: what is the difference?

The three bands Wi-Fi uses (2.4, 5 and 6 GHz) offer more width the higher they are, so higher bands are faster and less crowded, but at the same distance the received signal is weaker and thick walls such as concrete take more of it.

Try the bands around the house

Change the distance and the walls between the router and the laptop. Assuming the three bands transmit at the same strength, it works out each band’s lane width (channel width), received signal and theoretical top speed.

5 m away, no walls. 6 GHz is the fastest here (2,882 Mbps). Its received signal is 1/7 of 2.4 GHz, but its lane is 8 times as wide.

Picked band 5 GHz: it loses 61 dB over the distance and 0 dB in the walls. Its received signal is 1/5 of 2.4 GHz.

Lane width is the widest channel in that band. Received signal is compared with 2.4 GHz at the same spot. Top speed is the theoretical value for one spatial stream, and real speeds are lower.

How it works

  1. Speed is set by the width of the lane. A band is a range of frequencies that the regulator has opened for use without a licence, and a router uses one slot in it, called a channel. The wider the slot, the more it sends at once. The bands are 83.5 MHz wide at 2.4 GHz, 580 MHz at 5 GHz and 1,200 MHz at 6 GHz. So the widest channel is 40 MHz at 2.4 GHz, 160 MHz at 5 GHz and 320 MHz at 6 GHz, and the top speed of one spatial stream is 344 / 1,441 / 2,882 Mbps. Confirmed Source: Korea’s Technical Standards for Radio Equipment of Radio Stations That May Be Established Without Notification, Article 7 (band ranges); Wi-Fi Alliance, Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 7 Technology Overview, Table 2 and p. 5
  2. The received signal is weaker on the higher bands. At the same distance, 5 GHz receives about one fifth of what 2.4 GHz does (7 dB), and 6 GHz about one seventh (8.5 dB). This is not because the wave wears out faster in the air. It is because the shorter the wavelength, the smaller the area with which the receiving antenna collects the wave. Concrete walls add to this. One 10 cm wall takes 8 dB at 2.4 GHz, 14 dB at 5 GHz and 16 dB at 6 GHz. Thin sheets such as glass or plasterboard take about 1 to 3 dB, and the bands differ by less than 1.4 dB. A dB counts a ratio: 10 dB is 10 times and 3 dB is 2 times. Confirmed Source: ITU-R P.525-5, equations 3 to 6 (free-space loss); calculated from equation 43b and the example values of Table 3 in ITU-R P.2040-4 (implemented for this page). The wall figures are for a wave entering a smooth slab head-on
  3. So width is traded for reach. With the same channel width, the lower band, whose received signal is stronger, always comes out ahead. A higher band is faster when it uses a wide channel, and that advantage lasts only while enough signal is left. In this page’s calculation, 2.4 GHz became the fastest once three 10 cm concrete walls stood in the way at 5 m. In return, 2.4 GHz is crowded. Bluetooth and microwave ovens use the same band, and usually only three channels can be used without overlapping. Hypothesis Source: Shannon 1948, Theorem 17 (channel capacity); this page’s calculation (implemented for this page; it assumes the three bands transmit at the same power, and with other assumptions it is two to four walls); Bluetooth SIG, Bluetooth Technology Overview (the band Bluetooth uses); Wikipedia: List of WLAN channels and ISM radio band (non-overlapping channels and microwave ovens, secondary sources)

What the calculation showed

The loss on the three bands was calculated with the equations of the ITU-R Recommendations, and width and signal were combined into a speed with Shannon’s equation. The assumptions are the same power of 20 dBm (100 mW) on the three bands, antennas that receive evenly from every direction, a receiver noise figure of 7 dB, and one spatial stream. The speed is the smaller of the Shannon limit and the top speed in the Wi-Fi 7 document. The code was written for this page and checked with 46 tests.

  • When all three bands use a 20 MHz channel, 2.4 GHz was the fastest at every spot from 1 to 50 m with 0 to 4 concrete walls. Confirmed
  • With the widest channels, 6 GHz was the fastest wherever there was no wall. Without walls, the order did not change within 500 m. Confirmed
  • At 5 m with three 10 cm concrete walls, 2.4 GHz was the fastest (344 / 206 / 69 Mbps). With the power changed between 14 and 30 dBm and the noise figure between 3 and 10 dB, it took two to four walls. Hypothesis
  • With wooden doors, glass windows or plasterboard, 6 GHz stayed the fastest at 5 m even with twelve of them. Confirmed
  • Counting only what is lost inside the material, 6 mm of glass takes less than 0.2 dB, but with what bounces off its surfaces included it was 2 to 3 dB. In thin sheets, reflection is most of the loss. Confirmed

Common misconceptions

Myth5 GHz is fast because its frequency is high.

In factWhat makes it fast is the width of the channel. With the same 20 MHz channel, 2.4 GHz, whose received signal is stronger, was faster at every spot calculated. The higher bands simply have room for wide channels.

MythRadio waves of a higher frequency weaken faster in the air.

In factIn empty space, the strength of a wave that has spread out does not depend on frequency. What differs is the area with which the receiving antenna collects the wave, and it is smaller the shorter the wavelength. The loss in walls such as concrete is added on top.

Frequently asked questions

Which should I choose, 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz Wi-Fi?

In the same room as the router, or when the walls in between are thin, 5 GHz or 6 GHz has the advantage, because the channels are wider. The widest channel is 40 MHz at 2.4 GHz, 160 MHz at 5 GHz and 320 MHz at 6 GHz (Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 7 Technology Overview). At a spot behind several concrete walls, 2.4 GHz can have the advantage. In this page’s calculation, 2.4 GHz was the fastest at 5 m with three 10 cm concrete walls. The calculation assumes the three bands transmit at the same power, and with other assumptions it is two to four walls.

Why is 5 GHz Wi-Fi faster than 2.4 GHz?

Not because the frequency is higher, but because the channel is wider. The whole 2.4 GHz band is 83.5 MHz, so a 160 MHz channel does not fit. In the example of the Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 7 Technology Overview, the top speed of one spatial stream is 344.1 Mbps on a 40 MHz channel, 1,441.2 Mbps on a 160 MHz channel and 2,882.4 Mbps on a 320 MHz channel. In Shannon’s theorem (1948) too, the upper limit of what a channel can send without error is proportional to its width.

Why is 5 GHz Wi-Fi weak behind walls?

Two things add up. At the same distance, 5 GHz receives about one fifth of what 2.4 GHz does (7 dB, the free-space loss of ITU-R P.525-5). On top of that, concrete absorbs more of the wave the higher the frequency. Calculated with the equations and example values of ITU-R P.2040-4, one 10 cm concrete wall takes about 8 dB at 2.4 GHz and about 14 dB at 5 GHz. In thin sheets such as glass or plasterboard, the bands differed by less than 1.4 dB.

What is different about 6 GHz Wi-Fi (Wi-Fi 6E)?

Wi-Fi 6E is the name for devices that extend the features of Wi-Fi 6 into the 6 GHz band (Wi-Fi Alliance, 2020). The 6 GHz band is 5,925–7,125 MHz, 1,200 MHz wide (Article 7 of the Korean Technical Standards, US FCC 47 CFR §15.403). That width holds 59 channels of 20 MHz or 3 channels of 320 MHz. In return, at the same distance the received signal is about one seventh of that of 2.4 GHz (8.5 dB).

Why is 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi crowded?

Wi-Fi is not the only user of the 2.4 GHz band. Bluetooth uses 2.402–2.480 GHz (Bluetooth SIG), and household microwave ovens operate at 2.45 GHz (Wikipedia). Because the band is only 83.5 MHz wide, the channels used without overlapping are usually three: 1, 6 and 11.

Do higher frequencies weaken faster in the air?

Not in empty space. At distance d, the strength of the wave (its power flux density) is the transmitted power divided by 4πd², and frequency does not appear in it (ITU-R P.525-5, equation 3). What differs is the effective area λ²/4π with which the receiving antenna collects the wave (equation 4). With its shorter wavelength, 5 GHz has 0.20 times the area of 2.4 GHz, and 6 GHz 0.14 times.

What are the Wi-Fi channel widths 20, 40, 80, 160 and 320 MHz?

The width of one slot into which a band is divided. A router uses one slot at a time, and the wider the slot, the more it sends at once. Wi-Fi 4 uses up to 40 MHz, Wi-Fi 5 and Wi-Fi 6 up to 160 MHz, and Wi-Fi 7 up to 320 MHz (Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 7 Technology Overview, Table 2). Under the Korean rules, a device that uses 6 GHz indoors is limited to 5 dBm per MHz in 5,925–6,425 MHz and 2 dBm per MHz in 6,425–7,125 MHz. Because the limit is per MHz, the narrower the channel, the less power it can put out. At 5 dBm per MHz that is 18 dBm on a 20 MHz channel and 30 dBm on a 320 MHz channel.

Are Wi-Fi frequencies the same in Korea and the US?

The ranges of 2.4 GHz (2,400–2,483.5 MHz) and 6 GHz (5,925–7,125 MHz) are the same. At 5 GHz, Korea has 5,150–5,350 MHz and 5,470–5,850 MHz, while the US goes up to 5,895 MHz, 45 MHz more. The ranges where radar has to be avoided (5,250–5,350 MHz and 5,470–5,725 MHz) are the same. In Korea, 6 GHz devices that are also used outdoors use only 5,925–6,425 MHz, at 14 dBm or less. The Korean values were read from Ministry of Science and ICT Notice No. 2026-10, and the US values from FCC 47 CFR Part 15.
